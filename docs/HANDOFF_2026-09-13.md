## 0. Orientation in one paragraph

The question is whether public awareness of police violence changes what New
Yorkers ask EMS for. Outcome: mental-health / EDP call share at community-district
× day level, 59 districts. Treatment: a composite attention index (CAI-D) built
from Wikipedia pageviews on a basket of police-violence articles plus US Google
Trends. Design: stacked episode event study with episode-level randomization
inference, under a **confirmation freeze** — 2017–2020 is explorable ("discovery"),
2015–16 and 2021–24 are sealed ("confirmation"), and `FREEZE_ACTIVE = True`
enforces it through `freeze_guard.select_sample()`.

## 2. The result

**`PAPER_MASTER` §8 now exists.** It was empty by design until this session.

**The mental-health share does not move.** 29 discovery episodes, day −1
reference, joint test over days 0–7, 500 randomization draws:

| outcome | arm | first week | RI p |
|---|---|---|---|
| EDP share | OLS | −0.00123 | 0.695 |
| EDP count | PPML + offset | −0.01084 | 0.519 |
| narrow MH share | OLS | −0.00103 | 0.880 |
| narrow MH count | PPML + offset | −0.00615 | 0.826 |

**Both arms agree**, which matters: this project was caught once by a "2020
signature" that existed only in the denominator and reversed when counts were
modelled. It does not reverse here. Estimates are stable to the fourth decimal
across 14/28/60-day windows.

**One channel moves, and it is not the paper's.** Of 65 decomposition tests,
three survive Bonferroni (α = 0.000769) and all three are injury: share days 0–2
(+0.00144, p 0.00019), share days 12–14 (+0.00110, p 0.00071), log count days 0–2
(+0.00962, p 0.00050). Both placebos stay quiet (cardiac 0.222, asthma 0.097 at
their strongest), which is what lets the injury result read as signal rather than
as the largest of many draws.

**This reverses the GATE2 memo's emphasis.** That memo proposed a protest-injury
channel *alongside* a help-seeking story. On the rebuilt pipeline the protest
channel survives and the help-seeking story does not. §9 of
`GATE2_PRELIMINARY_RESULTS.md` says so rather than smoothing it over.

### The caveat that governs everything above

**It is a null of unknown resolution.** Power against the −0.005 minimum effect
of interest had not been computed when §8 was written. Until Phase H lands, "we
did not detect it" cannot become "it is not there", and §8 plus limitation 12 say
that explicitly. **Do not let this drift.**

## 4. What changed, and the reasoning behind each

Twenty-three commits. The through-line: **almost everything found this session
was a guarantee that nothing enforced**, and the failures cluster into three
families.

### Family A — a register or guard answering a question about the wrong object

- **P3.** `basket_artifact()` gave the broad basket arm its own files; nothing
  gave it its own *source ids*. Running it rewrote D1 and S11 in place, leaving
  **the primary arm with no provenance row at all**. `V.artifacts_current` passed
  throughout — it verifies the script behind every row that exists and cannot ask
  about a row that stopped existing.
- **P4.** The append-only history that would have shown P3 immediately **could not
  be parsed**: header frozen at 7 columns while later rows carried 9. Nothing in
  the repo read it.
- **P7.** `17_stacked_event_study.py` — the *primary estimator* — read no
  calibration at all. `30_confirmatory_run.py` did gate, but on **discovery's**
  artifact while writing C1 and C2 p-values.
- **N5.** `randomization_p` dispatched with `if scheme == "circular" else
  anchor_shift`. Renaming the scheme sent C1 down the else branch. **On any
  stratum with slack the same fallback would have produced an ordinary number
  from an uncertified null.** C1 failed loudly only because the anchor shift is
  arithmetically impossible there.
- **RI4.** Three findings filed under ids the register already used — see §6.

### Family B — the environment, which turned out to govern everything

**The container restarts every 30–70 minutes.** Four observed, each confirmed by
`uptime` reading `up 0 min`. Two destroyed long jobs that had written nothing: the
1000-sim calibration at ~45 min, and the discovery estimator at ~33.

The discovery run needs ~1.7 h. **It could never have finished.** Neither could
CP2's 1000-sim calibrations nor Phase H. This is not a property of the analysis;
it is a property of where the analysis runs, and the plan had recorded it only
for the calibration.

Both long paths are now checkpointed — `18_null_calibration.py` (N4) and
`event_study.randomization_p` (N6) — with per-index seeding so a resumed run
reproduces the run it resumed. **Verified by the same test in both places:
truncate the ledger to a fraction, restart, require the completed ledger to be
*exactly equal*.** That test is the only thing separating real resumability from
a cache that quietly changes the answer.

**Anything long added later must be checkpointed too.** The retry-wrapper pattern
is in `scratchpad/phase_g.sh`.

### Family C — statistical machinery that was wrong

- **RI1.** The randomization p-value was `k/n`, not `(1+k)/(1+n)`. Anti-conservative
  exactly where rejection decisions are taken, and it can return **zero** — which
  is not a p-value. Every stratum's artifact carried one. CP2's checklist had
  required the correct form all along.
- **RI2.** The uniformity test compared lattice-valued p-values against a
  *continuous* uniform, **directly beneath a comment saying not to**. Measured
  false-failure rate 6.4% at 1000 sims against a nominal 5%.
- **RI3 — the important one.** C1's circular shift slid across the seam between
  its two windows. C1 really has **10 episodes in 2015–16 and 5 in 2021**; the
  null reproduced that split on **12.4% of draws**, averaging 12.1 in the long
  block, because block 1 is 520 of 641 admissible days. The placebo designs were
  structurally unlike the design under test. Fixed with a **within-block** shift:
  the 10/5 split now holds on **100%** of draws, and discovery and C2 come back
  **bit-identical** because they are single-window.

### Findings attacked rather than accepted

Four were closed by measuring instead of reasoning. **In three of four, the
diagnosis held and the prescribed fix was wrong.**

- **T13** — registry name-matching. Its remedy (strip middle names) would have
  collapsed *James Craig Anderson*, murdered by civilians, onto four unrelated
  James Andersons and readmitted him to a police-violence index. The false
  negatives cost nothing measurable. **The real exposure was three published
  articles — one of them Breonna Taylor — whose basket membership rested on a
  name lookup alone.** Fixed with evidence the script already fetched and never
  used: the article's own first sentence. All three baskets rebuilt byte-identical.
- **E7 / E8** — episode labelling. Both said "state it as a limitation". Measuring
  first showed **45% of episodes had no label at all**, and where the labeller did
  produce a name it was usually *wrong*: 2020-09-22 was "Dijon Kizzee" while 87%
  of attention was Breonna Taylor's grand jury; 2017-06-16 was "Michael Brown"
  while 76% was Philando Castile, the day Yanez was acquitted. Every miss is a
  verdict, indictment or video release — which a death-date registry structurally
  cannot see. Labelling from the treatment series brings unexplained episodes to
  **zero**.
- **L7** — the April 2020 Wikipedia agent-class break. Real, correctly dated, and
  **in the wrong place**: 0.0009 SD across discovery (where the finding said it
  mattered) against **0.0856 SD across 2021–2024 — 90× larger**. That window is
  half the confirmation sample and all of C2. Its proposed fix (`all-agents`) was
  backwards; it adds spider traffic.
- **T6** — claimed 5× Trends precision decay. **Not present** on the component in
  the index: the relative quantization step is *finer* late (ratio 0.82) with 365
  non-zero days every year. The real censoring is in `trends_nyc`, already retired.

## 6. What I got wrong

Recorded because the pattern is more useful than the individual mistakes.

1. **I filed three findings under ids the register already used** (N1/N2/N3), so
   two rows shared an id with different statuses. **My first repair collided
   again** — R1/R2/R3 were also taken. Same mistake twice in five minutes. Now
   RI1–RI4 with `M.finding_ids_unique` enforcing it. Found while writing *this
   document*, which says something about when registers get read carefully.
2. **My first fix for the uniformity test made it worse** — 7.1% false failures
   against the 6.4% it replaced — because `kstest` computes its p-value assuming a
   continuous null whatever CDF you hand it. Caught only by measuring the fix.
3. **My first within-block shift made the split worse** — 3.9% against 12.4% —
   because I keyed block membership on *admissible days* rather than the window's
   calendar bounds, so the two edge episodes belonged to no block and hit a
   scatter fallback. Caught by the test, not by reading the diff.
4. **I introduced a 3.6× slowdown** by calling `admissible_days` once per window
   per draw. Caught by watching the sim rate, not by reading the diff. Now cached.
5. **I put six numbers into §4.1 with no claims register entry**, breaking the
   project's own standing rule the day after restating it. That gap is now
   `V.claims_cover_exhibits`.
6. **I overwrote the real event-study result with a 2-draw test run.** The
   no-downgrade rule written after the earlier 8-sim incident had only ever been
   applied to the calibration script.
7. **I launched a fetch that would have corrupted the broad-arm comparison** —
   re-running `11` without `--only wiki_ext` refetches GDELT, and any drift since
   the strict fetch would have entered the comparison *as a basket effect*. Caught
   by noticing the elapsed time was wrong, then killed. Now impossible:
   `config.BASKET_DEPENDENT_COMPONENTS`.
8. **I over-measured.** Three rounds on a uniformity test worth 1.5 percentage
   points of false-failure rate, while the item that actually decided the design
   (C1's scheme) waited. The user asked why it was slow and was right to.

**The pattern:** every one was caught by a *measurement*, not by review. Four were
caught by tests I had just written. None were caught by re-reading my own diff.

## 8. Conventions that are not obvious

- **All work on `analysis-rework`.** Not the session's designated `claude/…`
  branch. Standing instruction.
- **No Claude attribution anywhere** — commits, docs, artifacts. Standing
  instruction. I violated it once and amended.
- **Stage named paths, never directories.** `git add -A` swept unreviewed files
  into an unrelated commit twice, historically.
- **Every new check gets a defeat attempt** — make it pass with the defect intact,
  and fail with a valid alternative fix. This has caught something nearly every
  time.
- **Run the suite twice** after touching findings: `M.register_sync` runs last, so
  `M.status_honest` reads a stale artifact for it and lags one run.
- **`cd` to the repo root first.** Cwd drifts to `scripts/` and globs silently
  report 0 — this cost me a confused minute mid-session.
- **Claim `expr` is `eval`'d**: single expressions only, no assignments or
  semicolons. `pd`, `np`, `Path`, `PROJECT_ROOT` and `d` are in scope.
- **Claim templates must anchor only up to their own field** — embedding a sibling
  column's value makes a claim fail when an unrelated number moves.
- **`outputs/tables/` is gitignored.** Artifacts there are regenerable and are not
  in the repo; the claims register is what ties paper numbers to them.
