## Epidemiologist

*Overall.* Major revision. The design, the freeze discipline and the record are unusually strong for this literature, and every number I could check against the sealed files reproduces exactly — the reading transcript, the 14-of-24 sensitivity count, the decomposition survivors, the MDEs. What needs work is the reporting register: the paper never gives a confidence interval anywhere, so it describes itself as less informative than it is (its own intervals exclude the sustained effect it says it cannot exclude), while the one pattern it does narrate — the first-week rise-then-fall, carried into the Abstract — rests on eight coefficients not one of which is distinguishable from zero. Fixing those two, correcting the denominator-diagnostic sentence whose point estimates say the opposite of its p-values, stating the identifying assumption for a design with no comparison group, and cutting Methods back to the venue's budget would make this a strong contribution.

### E1 [major] (author decision)

**Where.** docs/PAPER.md — Table 1A; Results, "the smallest unadjusted randomization p among its four primary cells is 0.0115…"; Discussion, "What the reading says"; Abstract, "a sustained shift of that minimum is not excluded"

**Problem.** No confidence interval appears anywhere in the paper outside Figure 1's caption. The entire non-rejection is expressed through pre-freeze minimum detectable effects, and the paper therefore states four times that "a sustained shift of the minimum effect of interest, −0.005, is not excluded". The intervals implied by the numbers already printed in Table 1A contradict that. EDP share: C1 −0.00059 (SE 0.00136) → 95% CI −0.0032 to +0.0021; C2 −0.00019 (0.00114) → −0.0024 to +0.0020. Narrow MH share: C1 −0.00074 (0.00157) → −0.0038 to +0.0024; C2 −0.00067 (0.00133) → −0.0033 to +0.0019. All four exclude −0.005. So do both count arms: a −0.005 share shift against the discovery-period mean share of 0.0856 is about −0.058 log points, and C1 is +0.00945 (0.01623) → −0.022 to +0.041, C2 +0.00591 (0.01127) → −0.016 to +0.028. The pre-freeze MDE is an ex-ante design quantity computed against the asymptotic joint test; the realized precision on the estimand a sustained shift actually implies (the first-week mean) is better than that calculation assumed.

**Why it matters.** For a null result the interval is the finding. As written, the paper tells an editor it cannot speak to the effect size it declared it cared about, when its own estimates bound that size out on every primary cell. "Not excluded" reads as "uninformative", and a JUH editor triaging a null submission will weigh it that way. This is the paper underselling itself, and it is also simply an incomplete description of the evidence.

**Suggested change.** Add a "95% CI" column to Table 1A (computable from the printed mean and SE; no re-estimation). Keep the pre-registered sentence verbatim in the Discussion, then add beside it: "That bound is a design-based one, fixed before the lift against the asymptotic joint test. The estimates themselves are more precise than that calculation assumed: the first-week mean change in the EDP share is −0.00059 (95% CI −0.0032 to 0.0021) in C1 and −0.00019 (−0.0024 to 0.0020) in C2, and both intervals exclude a sustained first-week decline of −0.005, as do the count arms (−0.005 on a mean share of 0.086 is about −0.058 log points). The pre-registered reading is the study's conclusion and is unchanged; we report the intervals because they are what the estimates on their own bound." In the Abstract, replace "a sustained shift of that minimum is not excluded" with "the pre-registered reading does not exclude a sustained shift of that minimum, though the estimated intervals (share arm, within about ±0.003 of zero) do".

### E3 [major]

**Where.** docs/PAPER.md — Results, "Among the denominator diagnostics, C1's raw EDP count without an offset has a randomization p of 0.0115 while total dispatches have 0.5422"; Discussion, "C1's raw EDP count without an offset fell in the first week (randomization p 0.0115) while total dispatches did not (0.5422)"

**Problem.** Both sentences give p-values only, and the point estimates reverse what they imply. Table S3 records C1 EDP count without offset at −0.01180 and total dispatches at −0.01585: total dispatches fall by more, not less. "While total dispatches did not" states the opposite of the point estimate; what differs between the two cells is the joint statistic's distinguishability from its null, not the direction or size of the mean. The Discussion's own conclusion — "a small general contraction in dispatching that the design cannot separate from a mental-health-specific one" — is the correct reading, but the sentence placed in front of it argues against it. The same diagnostic is also the reason the share arm and the offset-count arm straddle zero (the denominator moves with the numerator), and the paper asserts the straddle twice without ever explaining it.

**Why it matters.** A reader taking the Results at face value concludes the first-week contraction is mental-health-specific — the opposite of what the diagnostic shows and the opposite of what the Discussion concludes two paragraphs later. This is the single most informative diagnostic in the paper for interpreting the share arm, and it is the one place where reporting p without the coefficient actively misleads.

**Suggested change.** Results: "Among the denominator diagnostics, C1's raw EDP count without an offset has a first-week mean of −0.0118 (randomization p 0.0115) and total dispatches −0.0159 (p 0.5422); in C2 the two are −0.0044 (p 0.3368) and −0.0100 (p 0.7481)." Discussion: "C1's raw EDP count without an offset fell in the first week (−0.0118, randomization p 0.0115); total dispatches fell by about as much (−0.0159), though its joint statistic is not distinguishable from its null (p 0.5422). The two point estimates are close enough that the design cannot separate a mental-health-specific contraction from a general one — and their closeness is why the share and offset-count arms straddle zero, the denominator having moved with the numerator."

**Fact-check.** accurate=False, keep=True. The comment's quotations and numbers are exact, and its core charge is right: both sentences report p only, Table 2 carries p only, and the Discussion's "while total dispatches did not" contradicts both the point estimates (total −0.01585 vs EDP −0.01180) and its own appositive ("a small general contraction in dispatching"). The suggested Results sentence is correct as written and does not disturb the frozen reading rule, which is itself stated in p-terms (PAPER.md:163-164, "a movement in total dispatches but not in the raw count is denominator-driven").

Two corrections to the Discussion half.

(1) The trailing clause — "their closeness is why the share and offset-count arms straddle zero, the denominator having moved with the numerator" — is wrong as a mechanism. Both primary arms carry the same denominator: the share is EDP/total dispatches and the count arm is PPML with total dispatches as a log offset (PAPER.md:152-155). A movement in a denominator common to both arms moves both the same way, so it cannot produce their opposite signs (share −0.00059, offset-count +0.00945). What the denominator does explain is the sign flip between the raw count (−0.0118) and the offset-count arm (+0.0095): total fell more than EDP, so the rate rose while the level fell. The sign split between the two primary arms is a functional-form/weighting difference (OLS on the level share weights district-days equally; PPML weights by volume) on top of the within-week cancellation the paper already describes. If the author wants the clause, it should read: "...and their closeness is why the offset-count arm is positive while the raw count is negative — the denominator fell slightly more than the numerator." The straddle between the two primary arms should not be attributed to the denominator.

(2) "the paper asserts the straddle twice without ever explaining it" is overstated. PAPER.md:273-276 does explain why both means are near zero ("the path is positive on days 0 to 2 and negative on days 3, 5, 6 and 7, so that the first-week mean, −0.00059, is a small fraction of any single day's coefficient"). What is unexplained is only the sign disagreement between the arms, and the comment's proposed explanation for it is not the right one.

**Disposition.** done: both diagnostics' coefficients in Results, Discussion and Table 2B

### E4 [major]

**Where.** docs/PAPER.md — Methods, "We conducted a quasi-experimental panel study — a stacked episode event study — of New York City emergency medical dispatches"; Methods, *Estimator*; Figure 2 caption, "treatment side only"

**Problem.** The design is named with terms that in this literature imply a treated/untreated contrast, but the exposure is national: every community district is exposed on the same day, and there is no comparison group. Identification rests entirely on within-window timing against day-of-week and episode × district levels, with no calendar-trend control inside a 29-day window. The randomization inference supplies a null distribution, not a counterfactual. The Methods never state the identifying assumption, and the only acknowledgement in the paper is the phrase "treatment side only" at the end of Figure 2's caption, which is never glossed.

**Why it matters.** This is the first question a JUH referee asks of an event study, and leaving it implicit invites the reading that some districts served as controls — a reading the words "stacked" and "event study" actively encourage. It also determines how much weight the cardiac and asthma outcomes must carry, since they are doing the work an untreated group would otherwise do, and the paper never says so.

**Suggested change.** Add after the first Methods sentence: "The exposure is citywide: every community district is exposed on the same day, so the design carries no untreated comparison group. Identification is within-episode and within-district across a 29-day window — the counterfactual for days 0 to 7 is the day-of-week-adjusted level the district's dispatches were running at in the 14 days before the episode, with the episode × district level held fixed. Cardiac and asthma dispatches, which should not respond to news, carry the burden that an untreated group carries in a difference-in-differences design; the falsification outcomes are therefore not a supplementary check but part of the identification argument." Extend Figure 2's caption: "treatment side only — there is no control series, because the exposure is national and reaches every district on the same day."

**Fact-check.** accurate=False, keep=True. The diagnosis checks out; two specifics in the comment do not, and both are in the suggested text, so the author should not paste it verbatim.

PAPER.md:164 gives the whole specification: "*y* ~ *i*(relative day, reference = −1) + B-HEARD exposure | episode × district + day of week" — the only absorbed terms are episode × district and day of week, no date effect and no calendar trend, over a 14-before/14-after (29-day) window, with the calendar date used only for clustering. Grepping PAPER.md and SUPPLEMENT.md for "untreated", "comparison group", "control group", "counterfactual" and "identifying assumption" returns nothing; the only "identif" hits in PAPER.md are "stable identifier" (l.116), "personal identifiers" (l.230), Limitation 1's "New Yorkers' own attention is not separately identified" (l.354) and RECORD 6.1 (l.458). Limitation 1 and the Discussion's "What the design cannot say" address national-vs-local attention (attenuation), not the absence of a comparison group, so the comment is right that the design's identifying assumption is never stated.
